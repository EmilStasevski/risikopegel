## Set-up

- 200 Home Credit test applicants, 20 per PD decile of the champion (lightgbm). Facts per applicant: the 4 features with the largest |TreeSHAP| contribution and their direction, plus the PD rounded to a whole percent.
- Protected-like attributes (age, birth date, family status, children, household size) are never handed to the text generator. In 10 of 200 applicants one of them was among the raw top 4; the next feature was used instead. The model still uses them; that is a finding for the model card, not hidden here.
- The **verifier** (`src/risikopegel/llm/verifier.py`) is deterministic. A text passes only if every named feature is in the facts, every sentence with a feature states the right direction, no number other than the PD appears, no forbidden wording (decisions, guarantees, legal claims, protected attributes) is used and at least 2 facts are named.

## Most common verifier errors (LLM few-shot)

- unrecognised factor wording (garbled label?): 53
- non-German characters: 11
- no clear direction stated for: 2
- numbers not in the facts: 1

Most few-shot failures are garbled factor names: when a text names two external scores, the quantised 4B model often corrupts the second one (for example "Auskunfte-Bei B", "Auskunfteigkeit C", or a Chinese character inside the word). The check for unrecognised factor wording was added to the verifier after a smoke test on 2026-11-21 and before the main run. Without it, these texts would have passed: a verifier is only as good as its list of failure modes.

## Findings

- **H6:** without the verifier, 9.0% of zero-shot texts (few-shot 28.5%) fail at least one check. With the verifier and up to 2 retries, 98.5% of LLM texts pass; 1.5% fall back to the template, so 100.0% of shipped texts are verified.
- **Cost of the LLM:** 1.44 s and 127 GPU J per verified explanation, against effectively zero for the template. The template is already faithful by construction. What the LLM adds is wording variety, at a measurable cost in risk and energy.

## Limitations

- The verifier checks consistency with the SHAP facts, not whether the SHAP attribution itself is a good explanation. It is strict by design: an ambiguous sentence counts as a failure.
- Readability uses a German Flesch adaptation with a heuristic syllable count.
- No human evaluation of tone or helpfulness. No claim of compliance with GDPR Art. 22 or the EU AI Act; the pipeline supports traceable, reproducible reason statements.

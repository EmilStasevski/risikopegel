## 2. Summary opinion

**7 findings: 1 High, 3 Medium, 3 Low.**

- **Discrimination:** the champion's advantage over the scorecard is real and robust. Test ΔAUC is +0.0226 [+0.0193, +0.0260], and the advantage holds on 3 other splits (0.0220 to 0.0229) and in every segment tested.
- **Calibration:** the main weakness is at grade level (see findings).
- **Main limitation:** the dataset itself. Without a time dimension, out-of-time performance is unknown.

**Opinion: fit for its stated purpose as a research benchmark. Not fit for credit decisions** until the High finding is resolved on data with a time dimension.

## 7. Conceptual soundness (qualitative)

- **Features:** application, bureau and previous-application aggregates built in dbt. Only rows dated before the application are used (dbt tests `not_after_application`). Gender is excluded from the inputs and used only for the group check.
- **Split:** stratified, not time-based. This is a documented exception (`docs/split_policy.md`) and the reason for F1.
- **Method:** gradient boosting with early stopping on valid. An interpretable alternative exists: EBM, test AUC 0.7764. So does a monotone-constrained one: test AUC 0.7728. Both are viable fallbacks if explainability requirements tighten.
- **Explanations:** exact TreeSHAP and reason codes. For jointly declined applicants, the top reason agrees with the scorecard's in 72% of cases.

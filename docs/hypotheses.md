# Pre-registered hypotheses

These were committed on 2026-11-20, before any model was run. They are copied word for word from PLAN §9. The verdicts get filled in at the end, from `results/` only.

| # | Hypothesis | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | A tuned boosted model beats the WoE scorecard on AUC by a small margin; the scorecard wins on stability (PSI) and is close on calibration after recalibration. | **confirmed** (2026-11-21, end of R2) | `reports/validation_hc_pd.md`, `reports/credit_hc_challengers.md`, `reports/credit_hc_tuning.md`, Home Credit test set, all six models tuned. **AUC:** the champion (LightGBM, chosen on valid) beats the tuned scorecard by ΔAUC +0.023 [+0.019, +0.026] (0.781 vs 0.759, Gini 0.56 vs 0.52). The gain holds on 3 re-splits (+0.022 to +0.023) and in all 9 segments tested. It is small in absolute terms but not noise. **Stability:** the scorecard has the lowest score PSI train→Kaggle (0.0006 vs 0.0043), although both are far below any action threshold. **Calibration:** the scorecard is not just close but better. Raw ECE is 0.0020 vs 0.0041. On the grade-level Jeffreys test the scorecard has 0 grades with PD too low, the raw champion 2 (1 at the Bonferroni level) and the isotonic-recalibrated champion 3. So the hypothesis holds, with calibration even more in the scorecard's favour than predicted. Caveat: one period, stratified split, no out-of-time test (validation finding F1) |
| H2 | Filtered historical simulation with GARCH-t passes Kupiec/Christoffersen in more periods than plain historical simulation or normal VaR; the normal model fails in stress. | **confirmed** (2026-11-21, end of R3) | `reports/market_var_backtest.md`, ECB FX/rates portfolio, 4,677 forecast days 2008-08 to 2026-11, 1000-day window. At 99 %, FHS GARCH-t passes Kupiec and conditional coverage in 17 of 19 calendar years, versus 10 for historical simulation and 11 for normal VaR. Over the full sample, FHS has 1.13 % exceptions (conditional coverage p = 0.60), historical simulation 1.35 % (p < 0.001) and normal 1.58 % (p < 0.001). In the GFC regime the normal model had 22 exceptions where 2.1 were expected; historical simulation had 19, FHS 2. The result holds for 250- and 500-day windows and for the equal-weight portfolio. Caveat: an FX/rates portfolio, not equities. FHS exceptions at 97.5 % still cluster (independence p = 0.002) |
| H3 | ML quantile models do not beat GARCH-based methods on VaR coverage. | **confirmed** (2026-11-21, end of R3) | `reports/market_var_backtest.md`. LightGBM quantile regression has 2.65 % exceptions at 99 % and LightGBM volatility + FHS 1.65 %; both are rejected by Kupiec (p < 0.001). They pass 10 and 12 of 19 years, against 16 or 17 for the four GARCH-type models. Diebold-Mariano on pinball loss shows both are worse than FHS (p < 0.001 and p = 0.018), and neither is in the 90 % Model Confidence Set. On the real series the direct quantile model does not even beat historical simulation on pinball loss (0.00887 vs 0.00877). Caveat: two simple ML designs, not an exhaustive search |
| H4 | On monthly macro series, ETS/ARIMA/seasonal naive are competitive with foundation models at a tiny fraction of the compute; the foundation models help mainly on series with few observations. | **refuted** in two of three parts (2026-11-21, end of R4) | `reports/macro_forecast.md`: 15 Eurostat series, 1,889 rolling-origin forecasts per model, 2016–2026. **Competitive: holds.** At h = 1 and 3, ETS, ARIMA and TimesFM 2.5 are statistically tied (DM TimesFM vs ETS p = 0.27 at both horizons; MASE 0.315 / 0.324 / 0.321 at h = 1). At h = 12 the foundation models are ahead: Chronos-Bolt 1.160 vs ETS 1.257, DM p = 0.044, the only significant gain. Seasonal naive is a weak reference here (most series are seasonally adjusted). **Tiny fraction of the compute: does not hold** on this CPU set-up. Energy above idle per forecast: ETS 0.29 J, Chronos-Bolt small 0.10 J, Chronos-2 0.43 J, TimesFM 2.5 1.78 J, ARIMA 4.11 J. Batched zero-shot inference is as cheap as per-series statsmodels fitting. **Help mainly on short series: not supported.** On the one short series (bankruptcies, 140 months) Chronos-2 ranks first and the three foundation models rank 1, 3 and 4 against 6 and 7 for ETS and ARIMA. But TimesFM is also best on the long series, so the advantage is not specific to short history. One short series is weak evidence. Caveat: revised data, not real-time vintages |

| H6 | LLM explanations without a verifier contain unfaithful statements at a non-trivial rate; the verifier plus a retry reduces it to near zero. | **confirmed** (2026-11-21, R5) | `reports/llm_explanations.md`: 200 Home Credit applicants (20 per PD decile), Qwen3-4B-Instruct (local Ollama), deterministic verifier. Without the verifier, 9.0 % of zero-shot texts fail at least one check (4.5 % name a feature not in the SHAP facts), and 28.5 % of few-shot texts fail, mostly with garbled factor names. With the verifier and up to 2 retries, 98.5 % of LLM texts pass (1.31 attempts on average). The other 1.5 % fall back to the template, so 100 % of shipped texts are verified. The verifier caught all 1,200 deliberately broken texts (6 mutation types × 200). Caveats: the verifier checks consistency with SHAP, not the quality of SHAP itself; one model size; no human rating |

Each verdict is one of: confirmed, refuted, inconclusive.
